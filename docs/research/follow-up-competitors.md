# Follow-up Competitors: Stress-Testing Finalist B's White Space

- **Date:** 2026-10-18 (all sources accessed 2026-10-18)
- **Question:** Does anyone already sell "AI drafts a follow-up for an unanswered inquiry or quote, the owner approves with one tap, it sends, and replies are tracked" to small Philippine service businesses at about ₱1,500/mo?
- **Builds on:** `competitor-analysis.md` (§1 conclusion 10 and §5 gap 1). Vendors covered there are not re-profiled unless the new evidence changes the conclusion.
- **Method limits:** the session's web-search budget ran out partway through. Items 1, 2 and 4 of the brief (global SMB follow-up tools, launches, Philippine vendors) were searched. Item 3 (WhatsApp-first SEA/LatAm/India) and item 5 (quote tools) were searched only partly. ServiceM8, Tradify, Fergus, Proposify, Birdeye, Relevance AI, Reply.io, Artisan, 11x, Qualified, Drift, Smith.ai, Verse, Follow Up Boss, Ylopo, Lofty, Rilla, Salesforge, Leadtrekker, Clientell, Sendbird, Yellow.ai, Chatbase and Voiceflow were **not verified** and are not scored. Most of these are US B2B outbound SDR tools or US real-estate CRMs, so they are unlikely to change the Philippine conclusion. That is a judgement, not a finding.

---

## 1. Verdict: weakened, not disproved

**The white-space claim is weakened.** I found no product that does B's full loop. But the most defensible-sounding parts of the claim (Philippine price point, Taglish, AI follow-up on Messenger, and the approval mechanic) are each already on sale. Confidence: medium.

1. **The price and locale part is gone.** Asikaso AI is a Philippine Messenger and Instagram AI agent priced at **₱1,499/mo**, the same as B's ₱1,500 hypothesis. Its Starter plan includes "up to 3 AI follow-ups per conversation." It replies in Taglish and Tagalog and targets clinics, resorts, agencies, contractors and real estate [1][2]. At least four more Philippine Taglish AI-reply products sell at ₱799–2,500/mo: Replia, AlonChat, NextGen AI and FreeUpToHours [4][5][6][7]. The earlier analysis said no local AI-agent SaaS turned up. That is now out of date for self-serve micro-SaaS, though it still holds for *funded* local players.
2. **The approval mechanic is not novel.**
   - GoHighLevel Conversation AI has a "Suggestive" mode in which AI drafts sit in the inbox composer for staff to approve, edit or discard [13][14].
   - Lindy holds "actions that write or send" for approval [40].
   - A US done-for-you vendor, Local Roots AI, sells one-tap owner approval of AI-generated quotes, followed by an automatic 2h/3d/7d/14d follow-up sequence [41].
3. **Quote-specific follow-up exists, but only in US field-service software.**
   - Jobber includes "automated quote follow-ups" from its Connect plan (US$99–139/mo) [25].
   - Hatch, now part of Yelp, runs AI follow-up on estimates for about US$600–1,500/mo [30].
   - Neither sells to Philippine businesses: Jobber's two-way texting covers only the US, Canada and the UK [25].
4. **What still looks unoccupied** is the *combination*:
   - detecting stale **quotes**, not just silent chats, including quotes that arrived by email or screenshot;
   - drafts grounded in the owner's price list;
   - **owner approval as the default**, not an opt-in mode;
   - sending through whatever channel is still allowed: Messenger inside 24h, email, SMS, or the owner copy-pasting it;
   - tracking the result to a booking;
   - at ₱1,500/mo for Philippine service businesses.
5. **That combination is a feature, not a moat.** Asikaso already has the customer base, price, language and AI follow-up, and offers an approval step for bookings [1]. Adding "approve follow-ups" and a quote status would take it weeks, not quarters. GoHighLevel agencies in the Philippines can configure something close today with Suggestive mode plus workflows.

**C6 (competitive room) recommendation: lower it from 4 to 3.** Details are in §6.

## 3. Notes on competitors scoring 3 or higher

### 3.1 Asikaso AI: closeness 4. The closest match found.

- **What it is:** an AI agent for Facebook Messenger and Instagram DMs.
  - It answers from the business's own information, captures leads, replies to comments and moves them into DMs, and alerts the team by Telegram.
  - It sends "follow-ups to bring customers back" [2][3].
  - The pricing page specifies "up to 3 AI follow-ups per conversation" on every plan, including the ₱1,499 Starter [1]. The page calls them "AI follow-ups". It does not say whether each one is generated per conversation or comes from a template.
  - A sample chat on the site shows a follow-up about 30 minutes after a customer went quiet [2].
- **Target:** the vertical list overlaps B's almost completely: hotels, resorts, clinics, salons, event venues, real estate, solar, insurance, agencies and contractors [2]. Replies are in English, Tagalog or Taglish, matching the customer's language [2].
- **Price:** ₱1,499 / ₱2,999 / ₱5,999 per month, 14-day free trial, annual plan with two months free [1].
  - Add-ons: Booking ₱499/mo (beta), with "automatic or wait for team approval"; 5,000 extra AI replies ₱1,499 [1].
  - Guided setup and maintenance are founder-led and included [3].
- **Approval:** documented **only for bookings** [1]. Follow-ups appear to be sent automatically. Confidence: medium, because the page does not say so explicitly either way.
- **Channels:** Meta only. Email, SMS and Viber are not mentioned [1][3]. Follow-ups therefore live inside Messenger's 24-hour window, the same limit B faces on Messenger.
- **Traction (vendor-published customer results, unverified):**
  - Vanity First dermatology clinic: 1,823 messages answered in 15 days.
  - RCDC Property Management: ₱244,600 in booking value during a one-month trial.
  - Lucky Siomai Siopao (Aug 2026): 16K+ messages and 356 leads [2].
  - An "early usage stats" block on the site shows zeros [2]. Contact is a Gmail address [3]. Read this as an early-stage, possibly solo-founder product launched around mid-2026. Confidence: low.
- **Why 4 and not 5:**
  - It is an inbound auto-reply agent that also nudges silent chats.
  - It does not track **quotes** as objects.
  - It does not ingest email or screenshot inquiries.
  - It does not make owner approval the default.
  - It cannot reach customers after the 24-hour window through email, SMS or owner copy-paste.
- **Why not 3:** it matches B on customer, price, language and the core "AI chases the silent lead" promise. A Philippine clinic owner comparing the two on a landing page would see "same thing, plus it answers my chats too."
- **Evidence:** vendor pages [1][2][3]. Evidence type: primary vendor. Confidence: high for price and listed features, low for traction.

### 3.2 GoHighLevel Conversation AI (Suggestive mode plus workflows): closeness 3

- **Approval:** Suggestive (copilot) mode drafts each AI reply in the inbox composer for a person to "approve, edit, or discard." Guides recommend it for medical, legal, real-estate and complex sales [13][14]. Auto-Pilot sends without review. This is the same draft-approve-send mechanic B is built on.
- **Follow-up:**
  - A Conversation AI workflow action sends one AI-generated message when a contact meets a condition, such as entering a pipeline stage or waiting N days.
  - A 3P guide found **no dedicated native "AI follow-up for unresponsive leads" feature** [13]. Agencies build it from workflows plus timers.
  - Whether the workflow action respects Suggestive mode is **unresolved** [14].
- **Channels:** Messenger, Instagram, WhatsApp, SMS (US A2P 10DLC), webchat and Google Business Profile. Email is not listed for Conversation AI [13].
- **Price:**
  - Agency plans US$97 / 297 / 497 per month [15].
  - Conversation AI about US$0.02 per message, or an unlimited add-on around US$49 per sub-account (3P) [13].
  - AI Employee US$50–97 per sub-account [15].
  - Philippine agencies resell it, often with a markup [13][15].
- **Why 3:** it has the mechanic and the channels, and Philippine GHL VAs can sell it to local SMEs today. But it is US-built, SMS assumes US carriers, set-up takes weeks [prior doc], and the effective price per SME is US$50–100+ once an agency is involved. Quote tracking is whatever the agency builds in pipelines.
- **Evidence:** 3P guides [13][14]. Confidence: medium. GHL's own help docs were not fetched.

### 3.3 FreeUpToHours (Baguio): closeness 3

- **What it is:** a done-for-you "AI Lead Automation for Messenger & Ads" by Oliver Valencia Sebastian, who also owns a 15-room transient house [7].
- **How it works:**
  - It captures inquiries from Messenger, ad comments, web forms and missed calls into a Google Sheets CRM with lead status.
  - It tags warm and "just browsing" leads and sends a daily lead report.
  - It sends **pre-written follow-up templates per tag** at 30 minutes, 24 hours and 7 days. "Just asking" leads get a two-day check-in.
- **Gaps against B:**
  - The templates are tuned in setup but not drafted per conversation.
  - No owner approval is mentioned.
  - The 7-day Messenger follow-up may conflict with Meta's 24-hour rule unless it is sent another way. The page does not say.
- **Price:** ₱2,499/mo bundled with the chatbot and Facebook ads management. "No setup fee. First 7 days free" conflicts with "Basic setup from ₱10k" on the same page [7].
- **Traction:** none published. "2 spots available this month" suggests a one-person service [7].
- **Why 3:** it sells the exact pain ("never lose a lead because it fell through the cracks") to the same customer at a similar price. But the follow-ups are templates, the business is done-for-you and capacity-limited, and the operator also writes a "how I run one for ₱500 a month" DIY guide [8]. That last point is evidence of how low the build barrier is.
- **Evidence:** vendor page [7]. Confidence: medium.

### 3.4 Kommo AI agent: closeness 3

- **Follow-up:** Kommo markets separate AI-agent roles, including a "follow-up agent." The product page says the agent "follows up automatically, keeping warm leads warm" [16]. Kommo's 2026 guide says AI sales agents "automate follow-ups across channels like WhatsApp, Instagram, live chat, and email" [18].
- **Approval:** not documented [16]. Treat it as autonomous.
- **Price and channels:**
  - US$25 / 35 / 45 per user per month, **billed for at least 6 months** (US$150 / 210 / 270 up front).
  - AI agents start on Advanced (up to 3).
  - Facebook is unlimited on all plans.
  - USD only; local-currency billing only through partners [17].
- **Why 3:** an explicit AI follow-up agent on Messenger-first CRM at US$35 per user is close in function and roughly in price for a solo owner (about ₱2,000/mo equivalent). The 6-month prepayment, CRM complexity, autonomous sending and lack of Taglish positioning keep it from scoring higher.
- **Evidence:** vendor pages [16][17][18]. Confidence: medium-high.

## 4. Other findings that matter

- **Meta has not shipped proactive follow-up.**
  - Meta's 2026-06-03 newsroom post describes the Business Agent only as responding, handing off and giving a morning briefing. It says nothing about the agent messaging first after a customer goes silent [21].
  - The WhatsApp Business product page does not mention follow-ups, reminders or pre-send approval [22].
  - A competitor's summary lists "closes sales and follows up" among the capabilities, without detail [23].
  - Separately, Meta tested consumer AI Studio bots that message users first within 14 days of a chat (2025) [24]. The technology exists inside Meta.
  - The "disprover" named in the earlier analysis (Meta shipping outbound follow-up) has **not happened as of 2026-10-18**. Confidence: medium-high.
  - **Not checked:** Marketing Messages Lite API and Advantage+ messaging, because the search budget ran out.
- **Quote follow-up as a category is proven in the US and missing in the Philippines.**
  - Jobber charges extra for it as a Connect-tier feature [25].
  - Hatch was bought by Yelp for about US$300M, closed in February 2026, and its pitch includes follow-up on recent estimates [30].
  - Housecall Pro sells follow-ups through a Pipeline add-on with no listed price [29].
  - This validates demand for the job in mature markets. It does not show Philippine demand.
- **Global AI SDR and follow-up tools are out of B's price band.** Conversica is about US$2,999/mo (3P) [32], Hatch US$700–1,500 [30] and Podium about US$399+ [31]. None targets the Philippines.
- **India's ultra-cheap DM tools set a price-anchor risk.** ReplyKaro sells rule-based IG and Messenger "Follow-Up DM" for US$3–9/mo [36]. A Philippine owner who just wants "send a nudge if no reply" has near-free options. B's value has to come from quote awareness, price-grounded drafts and approval, not the nudge itself.
- **No funded Philippine startup in this space surfaced.** The only nearby funding news was Sprouts.ai, an enterprise B2B sales-AI company with Kickstart Ventures participating, and Shoppable's Quotable AI, a B2B procurement quoting tool [10]. The 2026 Philippine entrants (Asikaso, Replia, AlonChat, NextGen, FreeUpToHours, QuoteKita) all appear self-funded and small. Confidence: medium.

## 5. What differentiates B, and what does not

### Does not differentiate (already sold by others)

| Claimed edge | Who already has it |
|---|---|
| ₱1,500/mo price point | Asikaso ₱1,499 [1]; Replia ₱799–1,999 [4]; NextGen ₱1,999 [6] |
| Taglish replies | Asikaso, Replia, AlonChat, NextGen, Bots at Work [1][4][5][6] |
| "AI follows up silent leads on Messenger" | Asikaso (3 per conversation) [1]; Kommo [16]; respond.io [19]; ReplyKaro (rule-based) [36] |
| Grounded in own prices | AlonChat ("grounded in your own prices, menu, and policies") [5]; Meta Business Agent learns from price lists [prior doc] |
| Draft, then human approves | GHL Suggestive mode [13]; Lindy [40]; Local Roots AI (quotes) [41]; respond.io AI Assist [20] |
| Quote follow-up as a job | Jobber [25]; Hatch [30]; Local Roots [41] (US only) |

### Plausibly differentiates (not found in any one product)

1. **Approval-first as the default posture, sold as risk control.** No Philippine vendor makes "nothing goes out without you" its pitch. They sell auto-reply volume. This matters if Philippine owners fear wrong prices; the earlier analysis notes Meta's own accuracy disclaimer. Demand is unverified.
2. **Quote as a tracked object.** "You sent ₱18,500 for a Boracay package 3 days ago, no reply" is different from "the chat went quiet." The quote can come from any channel, including an email thread or a pasted screenshot. No Philippine product found ingests quotes outside Meta inboxes.
3. **Reach past Messenger's 24-hour window through policy-safe routes.** These are email, SMS, and an owner copy-paste or human-sent route. Asikaso, Replia, AlonChat and the Meta agent are all Meta-only. Whether an owner-sent AI draft fits HUMAN_AGENT rules is still unresolved [prior doc §4].
4. **Outcome tracking from quote to booking**, rather than lead counts.

**Copyability:** all four are product decisions, not assets. The only durable advantages would be distribution (for example, an agency or association channel), a quote-and-booking dataset per vertical, or being first to be known as "the follow-up one" in a Philippine niche such as travel agencies or clinics.

## 6. Implication for C6 (competitive room)

- **Current score:** 4 (lots of room). That rested on absence of evidence: "no competitor found joins inquiry, quote, follow-up and booking for PH service SMEs."
- **Recommended score: 3.** The reasons:
  - A same-price, same-customer, same-language Philippine product with AI follow-ups now exists (Asikaso, closeness 4).
  - Four more Philippine Taglish AI-reply tools sit at ₱799–2,500.
  - The approval mechanic is commodity, available in GoHighLevel, which Philippine VAs already resell.
- **Why not 2:**
  - Nobody found combines quote tracking, multi-channel reach past 24 hours, and approval-by-default.
  - Every Philippine entrant is tiny and unfunded.
  - Meta still has not shipped outbound follow-up.
- **What would push it to 2:**
  - Asikaso or a peer adds approve-before-send and quote status.
  - Meta adds follow-up nudges to Business Agent.
  - GoHighLevel ships a native AI "stale lead" agent with Suggestive mode.
- **Evidence strength:** now based on direct vendor pages rather than absence of evidence. Confidence: medium.
